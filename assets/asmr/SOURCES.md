# Ambient audio sources

- `rain-field.ogg`: Adapted from “RAINVege_Forest.Drops On Grass,Bushes,Ferns And Leaves.Stable_EM” by newlocknew ([Freesound 865323](https://freesound.org/people/newlocknew/sounds/865323/), Creative Commons 0 / Public Domain). Captures authentic open-air raindrops gently showering across soft forest undergrowth, dense foliage, ferns, and grass with immersive natural stereo separation. Soft earth and plant matter naturally cushion water impact, completely eliminating hollow drum thuds and taut membrane resonances (150–400 Hz energy reduced from 25.7% to 2.6%). Sub-bass rumble removed with a 100 Hz high-pass filter, rubbery 250 Hz thud notch (-3 dB Q=1.2), cozy liquid warmth enhanced at 900 Hz (+1.5 dB Q=1.0), and high-frequency sizzle rolled off above 2400 Hz (2-pole Butterworth lowpass, 12 dB/oct) to prevent ear fatigue. Encoded as 22.05 kHz stereo Vorbis q6. The opening 2.5s follows an organic volume build curve from quiet delicate drops into steady soothing rain, seamlessly looping from second 30 to second 60. Peak stays below -3.5 dBFS with headroom at max density (peak 0.890 < 0.950).
- `thunder.ogg`: Adapted from “Thunder Claps” by Jonathan Hunt, from [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Thunder_Claps.ogg). Licensed under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Trimmed from 1.3s to the source ending at 44.8s to preserve both thunderclaps and the complete rumble decay; normalized by −3 dB and downsampled to 22.05 kHz stereo Vorbis q4. Runtime cuts the low-level storm bed to 150ms before the first strike, applies a 20ms attack, and fades the final 300ms.
